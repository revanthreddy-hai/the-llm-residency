What to notice

Spaces belong to tokens. The space before a word is stored inside the word’s token, shown here as a middle dot at the start of a chip, so “bank” at the start of a sentence and “ bank” after a space get different ids. Common English words are usually one token each; rarer words split into smaller pieces the vocabulary has seen more often. Numbers split in ways that follow frequency in the training data rather than place value. And in the sample sentence, both banks map to the same id: nothing in the token itself tells the model which meaning is intended.

Where the ids go next, and how the two banks eventually get told apart, is the subject of Week 1: Tokens, Context, and Embeddings.
