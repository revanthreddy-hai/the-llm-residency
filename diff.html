The Weekly Diff

What just moved in LLMs, filed where it belongs in the curriculum. The shelf holds the canon; the Diff holds the current — exactly ten links, never more, none older than a season. When something new earns a place, something old gives one up.

Working with Models · Weeks 1–8

  • Characterizing Interference Weights in a Tiny Language Model Blog · Turner, Wu & Batson, Anthropic · 2026-08 · W02

    Train a one-layer transformer, decompose it into virtual weights, and score each one by whether it does functional work or is interference left over from superposition. A concrete case study in what looking inside a model actually involves, at the smallest scale where the question makes sense.

  • State of Open Models: Summer 2026 Observations Blog · Hugging Face · 2026-08 · W07 W08

    A data-grounded read on the open-model landscape that separates release-day attention from sustained adoption: sub-1B models take 83% of all-time downloads, and Qwen leads community derivatives at 2.6× Meta’s footprint. Useful when picking a base model for the fine-tuning project, instead of following the leaderboard of the week.

Building One from Scratch · Weeks 9–16

  • Kimi K3: Open Frontier Intelligence arXiv · Kimi Team, Moonshot AI · 2026-07 · W11 W12

    Moonshot’s 2.8T-parameter open-weights release, and the report is specific about the stack: Kimi Delta Attention plus attention residuals, 16 of 896 routed experts active per token, a million-token context. A concrete case study of how a non-standard attention design and sparse MoE assemble into a complete frontier model.

  • Nemotron 3 Ultra: Open, Efficient MoE Hybrid Mamba-Transformer Model arXiv · NVIDIA · 2026-06 · W13 W14

    One of the most complete open tech reports of 2026: a 550B-total, 55B-active hybrid Mamba-attention run on 20T tokens, shipped with the data, recipe, and checkpoints — and the largest NVFP4 pretraining run documented to date, which makes the precision discussion unusually concrete. The released mixture percentages alone earn the data-week tag.

Inference and Operations · Weeks 17–22

  • Efficient Decode Context Parallelism with vLLM for Long Context Workloads Blog · NVIDIA & Red Hat engineers, vLLM blog · 2026-08 · W17 W21

    Tensor parallelism replicates the KV cache on every GPU, so MLA and GQA models hit the memory ceiling long before compute runs out. Decode context parallelism shards the cache along the sequence dimension instead — serving Kimi K2.6 on eight B200s, 6,091 tokens per second per GPU at concurrency 512, where the tensor-parallel baseline saturated.

  • The next generation of speculative decoding: DFlash and Spec V2 Blog · Z Lab, Modal & SGLang teams · 2026-06 · W19

    Speculative decoding moving from autoregressive drafters (EAGLE, MTP) to block-diffusion drafters that emit a whole draft block in one forward pass, paired with the engine-side scheduling needed to collect the win. Over 4.3× single-request throughput versus baseline on a 397B-A17B model.

  • Inside NVIDIA Rubin GPU Architecture Blog · NVIDIA · 2026-07 · W20

    The primary-source writeup for the GPU generation 2026 inference economics will be priced against: up to 288 GB of HBM4 at up to 22 TB/s, and a claimed 50 petaflops of NVFP4. Read it for the bandwidth and tensor-core numbers; file the throughput-per-watt claims under vendor benchmarks.

  • From Day 0 to Production SLAs: Serving GLM-5.2 on 24 NVIDIA B300 GPUs with vLLM Blog · DaoCloud team, vLLM blog · 2026-07 · W21 W22

    An unusually detailed end-to-end account of tuning a 744B MoE deployment against explicit SLA targets rather than peak throughput: prefill/decode disaggregated across 24 B300s — four 4-GPU prefill instances, one 8-GPU decode instance — with mean TTFT under 2.5s and mean TPOT under 20ms. The TPOT journey from roughly 40ms to 17ms is what constraint-driven inference tuning actually looks like.

Depth, Safety and Capstone · Weeks 23–26

  • DeepSeek-V4: Towards Highly Efficient Million-Token Context Intelligence arXiv · DeepSeek-AI · 2026-04 · W23

    The clearest published account of the recipe now standard in frontier post-training: train domain-specialist teachers with RL, then consolidate them into one model by on-policy distillation. In the million-token setting V4-Pro needs a tenth of the KV cache of its predecessor — the inference weeks will meet this report again.

  • CARE-Bench: Benchmarking Patient-Facing LLM Triage arXiv · Hua, Na & Ayubcha · 2026-08 · W15 W24

    Measures whether patient-facing models ask a clarifying question before recommending care — where asking was the correct action, only a third of prompted outputs preserved it. A worked example of designing a benchmark around the specific failure mode that matters, in this case triage.

How this list is made

Exactly ten links, always, and nothing older than a season: adding one means pruning one, and the count at the top records both. Candidates come from what the residency weeks actually touch, and every link is fetched and read before it lands here — publication dates checked on the page itself, primary sources only, nothing paywalled, nothing padded. Ten forces the only question that matters: what would this displace? What ages out but still deserves a place moves to the shelf. Format owes a debt to The Daily Diff.
